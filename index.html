Chapter 02 · Turning Points

SEVEN YEARS
THAT CHANGED
EVERYTHING

A handful of years reshaped the entire landscape. New titles flooded in, and crowdfunded prize pools shattered records. A global pandemic moved everything online, capturing more attention from gamers. Scroll through the seven moments that defined esports legacy.

Annual prize money distributed

2000
The First Sparks

Counter-Strike joins StarCraft: Brood War as one of the first major esports titles. Prize pools remain small, often under $20,000 per event, but organised circuits begin to form in South Korea and Europe. These early structures lay the groundwork for modern esports.

2006
Global Circuits Form

Total annual prize money surpasses $1.5M for the first time. The Cyberathlete Professional League and World Cyber Games bring international structure to Counter-Strike and Warcraft III. Regional scenes begin to connect into a recognisable global circuit, and the number of players nearly triples compared to the previous year.

2011
League of Legends Arrives

Riot Games launches the League of Legends competitive scene by funding prize pools and broadcasting the events. By 2012, total prize money reaches $5.2M. League of Legends later becomes the second-highest-earning title in the dataset, generating over $90M across 2,743 tournaments.

2013
Dota 2 Rewrites the Rules

Valve introduces crowdfunded prize pools through the Dota 2 compendium system. The International 2013 raises $2.87M from fans alone. Total annual prize money jumps to $17M in a single year, marking the largest increase in the dataset. Dota 2 goes on to earn $315M overall, making it the most lucrative esport recorded.

2018
Fortnite & the $100M Bet

Epic Games commits $100M to Fortnite prize pools, helping launch the battle royale era. Annual prize money reaches a record $123M. The number of unique winners rises to 19,120, more than triple the previous year, as the genre attracts a large wave of new competitors.

2020
The Pandemic Paradox

COVID-19 leads to the cancellation of live events worldwide, but esports continued to operate. Prize money drops to $51M, its lowest level since 2017, while online tournaments remain high at 228 events. Games like Fortnite and PUBG Mobile, which are built for remote play, helped sustain activity. The share of prize money awarded online reaches its highest point.

2023
A New Equilibrium

Prize money stabilises around $70M. This is below the 2018 peak, but it is spread across a record 258 tournaments and 13,600 winners. The impact of any single breakout title is reduced. The ecosystem becomes broader, more diverse, and less dependent on a handful of games.

Chapter 04 · The Genre Wars

WHICH GAMES
TOOK THE MONEY

MOBAs generated the most prize money of any genre, with over $455M in total. This was largely driven by Dota 2 and League of Legends. First-person shooters lead in tournament count and player participation, with CS:GO alone spanning more than 8,000 events.
