Chapter 02 · Turning Points

THE MOMENTS
THAT CHANGED
EVERYTHING

A handful of pivotal years reshaped what esports could be — in prize money, in cultural reach, and in who got to compete.

Prize pool milestone

2001
The First Sparks

Counter-Strike and StarCraft dominate the earliest prize pools. Total annual prize money is under $3M. Tournaments are regional, grassroots, and largely unknown to mainstream audiences.

2006
The WCG Era

The World Cyber Games draws over 70 countries. Prize totals cross $10M globally for the first time. RTS and FPS games reign. A faint outline of an industry begins to form.

2011
League Arrives

League of Legends launches its competitive circuit. The MOBA genre explodes. Total prize pools surge past $30M. A new generation of titles — built for esports — begins its takeover.

2013
Dota 2 & The International

Valve's crowdfunded prize model debuts — and redefines what a prize pool can be. The International breaks $2.8M. The following years see it hit $10M, then $20M, then $40M. Nothing would be the same.

2018
Fortnite Changes Everything

Epic Games commits $100M to Fortnite tournaments. Battle royale explodes as a genre. Casual gaming and elite competition blur. Over 40 million players enter competitive events.

2020
The Pandemic Pivot

COVID-19 shuts down physical venues — but online prize pools surge. Esports becomes one of the only live sports still running. A genre built for screens thrives when screens are all there is.
